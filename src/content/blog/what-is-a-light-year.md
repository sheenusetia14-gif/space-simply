A light-year is a distance, not a time. It is how far light travels in one year, which is about 9.5 lakh crore kilometres (9.5 trillion km).

## Why do we need such a big ruler?

On Earth, we measure distances in kilometres. Delhi to Mumbai is about 1,400 km by road. That works fine.

But space is so enormous that kilometres become useless. The nearest star after the Sun is about **40 lakh crore kilometres** away (40 trillion km). Numbers like that are hard to write and impossible to picture. So astronomers use a bigger ruler: the light-year.

## How fast is light?

Light is the fastest thing in the universe. It travels about **3 lakh kilometres every second** (300,000 km/s).

That's fast enough to go around the Earth about seven and a half times in one second. Now imagine it keeps going like that, without stopping, for a whole year. The distance it covers is one light-year.

## Some distances in "light-time"

Because light is so fast, it's also handy to talk about light-seconds and light-minutes:

- **The Moon** is about 1.3 light-seconds away. Light from the Moon reaches you in just over a second.
- **The Sun** is about 8 light-minutes away. Sunlight takes around 8 minutes to reach Earth.
- **Proxima Centauri**, the nearest star after the Sun, is about 4.2 light-years away.
- **The Andromeda Galaxy**, our big galaxy neighbour, is about 2.5 million light-years away.

## Looking at stars is looking at the past

Here's the magical part. Since light takes time to travel, you never see a star as it is *right now*. You see the light that left it long ago.

When you look at the Sun (please don't look directly!), you see it as it was about 8 minutes ago. If the Sun suddenly vanished, we wouldn't know for 8 minutes.

> The light from the Andromeda Galaxy that reaches your eyes tonight left it about 2.5 million years ago, long before modern humans existed on Earth. You are literally seeing ancient light.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a light-year a measure of time?

No, even though it has "year" in the name. It measures distance. The "year" just tells you how long the light was travelling.

### Can we travel one light-year?

Not with today's technology. Even our fastest spacecraft would take well over a thousand years to cover a single light-year.

### Why not just use kilometres?

You could, but the numbers get silly. Saying "4.2 light-years" is much easier than "about 40 lakh crore kilometres", and it also tells you how long the light took to reach us.

### Can I see Andromeda with my own eyes?

Yes, from a dark place away from city lights, it looks like a faint, fuzzy smudge. It is the farthest thing most people can see without a telescope.
